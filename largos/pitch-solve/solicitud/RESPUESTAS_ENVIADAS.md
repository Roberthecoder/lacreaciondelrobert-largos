### What specific problem are you solving? (Required) (Word Limit: 200)

Jalisco's tequila industry leaves behind about 915,000 tonnes of agave bagasse a year, the fibre left after the sugars are extracted (Warren-Vega et al., 2025). Most of it is burned, dumped or left in open piles to rot. Distilleries treat it as a cost: the general coordinator of a distillery I interviewed pays 200 to 500 pesos per tonne to haul it away, and a compost operator told me authorized disposal sites are scarce. The people exposed live in the rural towns of the agave region, where the tequila chain sustains more than 100,000 jobs (CNIT): burning releases carbon dioxide, black carbon and particulates, and open piles release methane and leachates and shelter flies, pests and rodents. Globally, open burning of forests, grasslands and crop residues produces about a third of black carbon emissions, about 300,000 tonnes a year from crop residues alone (Bond et al., 2013), and Latin America repeats the pattern with sugarcane bagasse, coffee residues and corn stover. Meanwhile, a restaurant supplier in my network reports that gourmet mushrooms such as shiitake and lion's mane often come from other states or are imported.

### What is your solution? (Required) (Word Limit: 200)

Fungi Mati grows gourmet mushrooms on pelletized agave bagasse, hydrated, pasteurized and supplemented with 20 percent soybean hull (the next protocol under test), then inoculated with Pleurotus ostreatus, P. ostreatus var. columbinus or pink oyster, with lion's mane next. In four to six weeks the fibre becomes food, and the spent substrate returns to the soil as compost. The Zapopan home farm is being set up with low-cost equipment bought in September–October 2026: a 39-litre pressure canner as autoclave, a laminar flow hood, and a fruiting room under negative pressure with filtered intake, an inline extractor and humidistat-controlled ultrasonic humidification. Beyond the farm, I run the strain collection at the Carrillo Biorefinery Lab of Tec de Monterrey, led by Dr. Danay Carrillo Nieves, where an approved breeding project will cross single-spore monokaryons of two parental Pleurotus strains to test whether a strain better adapted to bagasse can be obtained. I also built an AI tutor in Botpress on OpenAI's GPT models, embedded in Google Sites, with guidance and early support from Equitech Futures, and tested it in a 50-student course against a traditionally taught control group; its next version, built on Claude, is live on our website.

### Who does your solution serve, and in what ways will the solution impact their lives? (Required) (Word Limit: 200)

Three groups. First, distilleries and agave growers. Bagasse costs them money and space, and they are underserved because their only routes are burning, dumping or low-value compost: one distillery coordinator I interviewed pays 200 to 500 pesos per tonne for hauling. Fungi Mati turns that cost into a supply relationship, starting with distilleries, whose residue is concentrated, since field growers did not answer my outreach. Their rural neighbours benefit too: a woman living a kilometre from a distillery has grown used to the smell and flies, so the change is fewer fires and rotting piles near homes. Second, restaurants and households in Guadalajara and nearby areas, served today by gourmet mushrooms that often travel from other states or are imported. They get fresh mushrooms grown locally, through a restaurant distributor and a well-connected saleswoman in my network and a 700-member gourmet food community. Third, people who want to learn to grow without a lab: my 2026 PrepaTec pilot reached 85 students, teachers, staff and visitors; in January I helped organize a course for 50 young women and taught part of it; and I sell grow kits and courses built around a pressure cooker and a plastic tent.

## More About Your Solution and Business Model

### What makes your solution innovative? (Word Limit: 300)

Most mushroom farms in Mexico grow on straw or sawdust pellets and treat the substrate as a commodity. Fungi Mati starts from the opposite end: one of the region's most abundant residues, agave bagasse, which today is burned, dumped or composted slowly, becomes gourmet mushrooms in four to six weeks. That speed is the core of the solution. In the time compost takes to half-break down, the same fibre has already become food that sells by the kilogram, and the spent substrate still returns to the soil. Pelletized bagasse makes it repeatable: pellets standardize moisture and particle size, store for months, and at 22,000 to 24,000 pesos per tonne cost about half of a conventional substrate. Of the ten existing bagasse uses I benchmarked, from adobe bricks and briquettes to bioplastics and compost, none turns it into a high-value food through a model a small producer can run. The approach is already moving the local market: persuaded by the case for mushrooms, my pellet supplier launched a mushroom pellet line in October 2026, for now of soybean hull and oak wood, with an agave-bagasse version as the next step. Because it needs no industrial plant, it can be copied by a grower in any town near a distillery, and knowledge travels with it through courses, kits and an AI tutor built with guidance and early support from Equitech Futures. If enough growers adopt it, bagasse stops being a disposal cost and becomes a traded input that distilleries can sell. As an exploratory line, at the Carrillo Biorefinery Lab with Dr. Danay Carrillo Nieves, I will test whether crossing single-spore monokaryons of two Pleurotus parental strains yields a strain better adapted to bagasse; if it works, the method travels to spent coffee grounds, corn stover or sugarcane bagasse.

### Describe your solution's tech stack and how you know that it works. (Word Limit: 300)

Solid-state fermentation of lignocellulose by white-rot fungi: Pleurotus enzymes degrade lignin and cellulose, and the fibre becomes fruiting bodies. Steps: hydration of pellets, pasteurization, inoculation with grain spawn produced on popcorn in a 39-litre pressure canner, incubation in bags, and fruiting in a negative-pressure room with filtered intake, an inline extractor and humidistat-controlled ultrasonic humidification. My own results: about 60 percent biological efficiency on sawdust pellets, and about 50 percent over two weighed flushes on unsupplemented agave bagasse pellets (triplicate, P. ostreatus var. columbinus); 20 percent soybean hull is the next step. Strain work uses classical genetics: Pleurotus ostreatus is tetrapolar, so crosses between sibling monokaryons are compatible only about 25 percent of the time; crossing two different parental strains in a factorial design escapes that limit, and compatibility is confirmed by clamp connections on confrontation plates. Biological efficiency is compared against the parental strains with one-way ANOVA. Published work supports the approach: Pleurotus colonizes and fruits on agave bagasse (Larios-Ulloa and Ramírez-Muñoz, 2024; Arias García and Soto Velazco, 2025), with biological efficiencies of 41.2 to 65.7 percent (Lagunes-Reyes et al., 2023) and 56.7 percent for P. djamor on a bagasse and barley straw mix (Cruz-Moreno et al., 2023); the tetrapolar mating system is described by Eger (1978); monokaryon crosses of P. ostreatus have produced hybrids that outyield both parents (Rana et al., 2024); and yield in Pleurotus is heritable (Avin et al., 2016). The AI tutor is a Botpress chatbot running on OpenAI's GPT models, embedded in Google Sites, built with guidance and early support from Equitech Futures and tested against traditional teaching in a 50-student course; its next version, built on Claude, is live on our website. Everything is built so that a grower with a pressure cooker and a plastic tent can reproduce it.
